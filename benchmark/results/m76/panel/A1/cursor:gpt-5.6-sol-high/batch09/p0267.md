## Prompt
Denali and Nate work for a dog walking business and are paid for each dog they walk. Denali is responsible for $16$ dogs and Nate is responsible for $12$ dogs. Under the company's new policy, they will be assigned or unassigned new dogs in groups of $x$ dogs. The ratio of Denali's pay to Nate's pay would be the same if Denali started walking $4x$ more dogs and Nate stayed at $12$ dogs or if $x$ of Nate's dogs were reassigned to Denali. Find $x$ if $x\neq0$.

## Response A
# Finding x for the Dog Walking Business

## Setting Up the Scenarios

Since they're paid per dog, pay is proportional to the number of dogs walked.

**Scenario 1:** Denali walks 4x more dogs, Nate stays at 12.
- Denali: 16 + 4x dogs
- Nate: 12 dogs
- Pay ratio: $\dfrac{16 + 4x}{12}$

**Scenario 2:** x of Nate's dogs are reassigned to Denali.
- Denali: 16 + x dogs
- Nate: 12 − x dogs
- Pay ratio: $\dfrac{16 + x}{12 - x}$

## Setting the Ratios Equal

$$\frac{16 + 4x}{12} = \frac{16 + x}{12 - x}$$

## Solving

Cross-multiplying:

$$(16 + 4x)(12 - x) = 12(16 + x)$$

Expanding the left side:

$$192 - 16x + 48x - 4x^2 = 192 + 12x$$

$$192 + 32x - 4x^2 = 192 + 12x$$

Subtracting 192 from both sides and simplifying:

$$32x - 4x^2 = 12x$$

$$20x - 4x^2 = 0$$

$$4x(5 - x) = 0$$

This gives $x = 0$ or $x = 5$. Since $x \neq 0$:

## Verification

- **Scenario 1:** Denali walks 16 + 20 = 36 dogs; Nate walks 12 dogs → ratio = 36/12 = **3**
- **Scenario 2:** Denali walks 16 + 5 = 21 dogs; Nate walks 12 − 5 = 7 dogs → ratio = 21/7 = **3** ✓

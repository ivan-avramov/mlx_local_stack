## Prompt
Let $S$ be the set of points $(a,b)$ with $0 \le a,$ $b \le 1$ such that the equation
\[x^4 + ax^3 - bx^2 + ax + 1 = 0\]has at least one real root.  Determine the area of the graph of $S.$

## Response A
Let

\[
P(x)=x^4+ax^3-bx^2+ax+1.
\]

Since the constant term is \(1\), \(x=0\) is not a root. For any nonzero root \(x\), divide by \(x^2\):

\[
x^2+ax-b+\frac{a}{x}+\frac1{x^2}=0.
\]

Group terms:

\[
\left(x^2+\frac1{x^2}\right)+a\left(x+\frac1x\right)-b=0.
\]

Let

\[
t=x+\frac1x.
\]

Then

\[
x^2+\frac1{x^2}=t^2-2,
\]

so the equation becomes

\[
t^2+at-b-2=0.
\]

For real \(x\), the quantity \(t=x+\frac1x\) must satisfy

\[
t\in (-\infty,-2]\cup[2,\infty).
\]

Conversely, any such \(t\) gives a real \(x\). Therefore the original quartic has a real root iff the quadratic

\[
t^2+at-b-2=0
\]

has a root \(t\) with \(|t|\ge 2\).

Its roots are

\[
t=\frac{-a\pm\sqrt{a^2+4b+8}}2.
\]

Let the positive root be

\[
t_+=\frac{-a+\sqrt{a^2+4b+8}}2.
\]

We check whether \(t_+\ge 2\):

\[
\frac{-a+\sqrt{a^2+4b+8}}2\ge 2
\iff
\sqrt{a^2+4b+8}\ge a+4.
\]

Squaring gives

\[
a^2+4b+8\ge a^2+8a+16
\iff
b\ge 2a+2.
\]

But \(0\le a,b\le 1\), so this is impossible. Thus the positive root never reaches \(2\).

Now consider the negative root

\[
t_-=\frac{-a-\sqrt{a^2+4b+8}}2.
\]

We need

\[
t_-\le -2.
\]

This is equivalent to

\[
\frac{-a-\sqrt{a^2+4b+8}}2\le -2
\]

or

\[
\sqrt{a^2+4b+8}\ge 4-a.
\]

Since \(0\le a\le 1\), \(4-a>0\), so we may square:

\[
a^2+4b+8\ge (4-a)^2
\]

\[
a^2+4b+8\ge a^2-8a+16
\]

\[
4b\ge 8-8a
\]

\[
b\ge 2-2a.
\]

Therefore

\[
S=\{(a,b)\in[0,1]^2:b\ge 2-2a\}.
\]

Inside the unit square, the line \(b=2-2a\) intersects the boundary at

\[
(a,b)=\left(\frac12,1\right)
\]

and

\[
(a,b)=(1,0).
\]

Thus \(S\) is the right triangle with vertices

\[
\left(\frac12,1\right),\quad (1,1),\quad (1,0).
\]

Its legs have lengths \(\frac12\) and \(1\), so its area is

\[
\frac12\cdot \frac12\cdot 1=\frac14.
\]

\[
\boxed{\frac14}
\]

## Response B
We need the area of the region

\[
S=\{(a,b):0\le a,b\le 1,\ \text{and }x^4+ax^3-bx^2+ax+1=0\text{ has a real root}\}.
\]

Let

\[
P(x)=x^4+ax^3-bx^2+ax+1.
\]

Since \(P(0)=1\), any real root must have \(x\neq 0\). Divide the equation by \(x^2\):

\[
x^2+ax-b+\frac{a}{x}+\frac1{x^2}=0.
\]

Group terms:

\[
\left(x^2+\frac1{x^2}\right)+a\left(x+\frac1x\right)-b=0.
\]

Let

\[
t=x+\frac1x.
\]

For real \(x\neq 0\), we have

\[
t\in (-\infty,-2]\cup [2,\infty).
\]

Also,

\[
x^2+\frac1{x^2}=t^2-2.
\]

Thus the equation becomes

\[
t^2-2+at-b=0,
\]

or

\[
b=t^2+at-2.
\]

So for fixed \(a\in[0,1]\), we need to know which \(b\in[0,1]\) can be written as

\[
b=f_a(t)=t^2+at-2
\]

for some \(t\in (-\infty,-2]\cup[2,\infty)\).

---

### 1. The branch \(t\ge 2\)

For \(t\ge 2\) and \(a\ge 0\),

\[
f_a(t)=t^2+at-2\ge t^2-2\ge 2.
\]

But \(b\le 1\), so this branch can never produce a valid \(b\).

---

### 2. The branch \(t\le -2\)

For \(t\le -2\),

\[
f_a'(t)=2t+a.
\]

Since \(0\le a\le 1\),

\[
2t+a\le -4+1=-3<0.
\]

So \(f_a(t)\) is decreasing on \((-\infty,-2]\). Its minimum occurs at \(t=-2\):

\[
f_a(-2)=4-2a-2=2-2a.
\]

As \(t\to -\infty\), \(f_a(t)\to\infty\). Therefore the range of \(f_a\) on \(t\le -2\) is

\[
[2-2a,\infty).
\]

We need this range to intersect \([0,1]\). Thus

\[
2-2a\le 1,
\]

which gives

\[
a\ge \frac12.
\]

For such \(a\), the allowable \(b\)-values are

\[
2-2a\le b\le 1.
\]

Therefore

\[
S=\left\{(a,b): \frac12\le a\le 1,\ 2-2a\le b\le 1\right\}.
\]

This is a right triangle in the unit square with vertices

\[
\left(\frac12,1\right),\quad (1,0),\quad (1,1).
\]

Its base is \(1\) and its height is \(\frac12\), so its area is

\[
\frac12\cdot 1\cdot \frac12=\frac14.
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\int_{1/2}^{1}\left(1-(2-2a)\right)\,da
=\int_{1/2}^{1}(2a-1)\,da
=\frac14.
\]

Thus the area of \(S\) is

\[
\boxed{\frac14}
\]

## Prompt
The sum of the first $n$ terms in the infinite geometric sequence $\left\{\frac{1}{4},\frac{1}{8},\frac{1}{16},\dots \right\}$ is $\frac{255}{512}$. Find $n$.

## Response A
The sequence is geometric with

\[
a=\frac14,\qquad r=\frac12.
\]

The sum of the first \(n\) terms is

\[
S_n=a\frac{1-r^n}{1-r}.
\]

Substitute \(a=\frac14\) and \(r=\frac12\):

\[
S_n=\frac14\cdot \frac{1-\left(\frac12\right)^n}{1-\frac12}
\]

\[
S_n=\frac14\cdot \frac{1-\left(\frac12\right)^n}{\frac12}
\]

\[
S_n=\frac12\left(1-\left(\frac12\right)^n\right).
\]

We are given

\[
S_n=\frac{255}{512}.
\]

So

\[
\frac12\left(1-\left(\frac12\right)^n\right)=\frac{255}{512}.
\]

Multiply both sides by \(2\):

\[
1-\left(\frac12\right)^n=\frac{255}{256}.
\]

Thus

\[
\left(\frac12\right)^n=1-\frac{255}{256}=\frac{1}{256}.
\]

Since

\[
\frac{1}{256}=\left(\frac12\right)^8,
\]

we have

\[
n=8.
\]

## Prompt
Daniel works at an electronics store, and he claims that the popularity of a television (measured in number of sales) is inversely proportional to its cost. If 15 customers buy a television that costs $\$$1500, according to Daniel's theory, how many customers would buy a television that costs $\$$2500?

## Response A
# Inverse Proportionality Problem

## Setting Up the Relationship

Since popularity (number of sales) is **inversely proportional** to cost, we have:

$$\text{sales} \times \text{cost} = k \quad \text{(constant)}$$

## Finding the Constant

Using the given data (15 customers buy a $1500 television):

$$k = 15 \times 1500 = 22{,}500$$

## Solving for the New Number of Customers

For a television costing $2500:

$$\text{sales} \times 2500 = 22{,}500$$

$$\text{sales} = \frac{22{,}500}{2500} = 9$$

## Answer

$$\boxed{9}$$

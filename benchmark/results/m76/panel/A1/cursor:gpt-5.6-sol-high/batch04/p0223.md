## Response A
# Finding the Shape Described by $z = c$

## Step 1: Understand Cylindrical Coordinates

In cylindrical coordinates $(r, \theta, z)$:
- $r$ = radial distance from the $z$-axis
- $\theta$ = angle in the $xy$-plane
- $z$ = height along the $z$-axis (same as in Cartesian coordinates)

## Step 2: Analyze the Equation $z = c$

The equation $z = c$ states that **$z$ is fixed** at the constant value $c$, while $r$ and $\theta$ are **free to take any values**:
- $r \geq 0$ (any non-negative value)
- $\theta \in [0, 2\pi)$ (any angle)

## Step 3: Interpret the Geometry

Since $z$ is constant and both $r$ and $\theta$ vary freely, every point in the $xy$-plane at height $z = c$ is included. This traces out an entire **horizontal plane** parallel to the $xy$-plane, at height $c$.

Converting to Cartesian coordinates confirms this: since $z$ is the same in both systems, $z = c$ is simply a plane.

## Answer

$$\boxed{C}$$

For how many positive integers $n>1$ is it true that $2^{24}$ is a perfect $n^{\text{th}}$ power?

## Response A
# Finding Perfect $n$-th Powers of $2^{24}$

## Setting Up the Problem

For $2^{24}$ to be a perfect $n$-th power, there must exist a positive integer $k$ such that:

$$k^n = 2^{24}$$

Since $2^{24}$ is a power of 2, $k$ must itself be a power of 2. Let $k = 2^m$ where $m$ is a positive integer. Then:

$$(2^m)^n = 2^{24} \implies 2^{mn} = 2^{24} \implies mn = 24$$

## Counting Valid Values of $n$

We need **positive integers** $m$ and $n$ with $n > 1$ and $mn = 24$. This means $n$ must be a divisor of 24 that is greater than 1.

**Factorizing:** $24 = 2^3 \cdot 3^1$

**Total divisors:** $(3+1)(1+1) = 8$

The divisors of 24 are:
$$1,\; 2,\; 3,\; 4,\; 6,\; 8,\; 12,\; 24$$

Excluding $n = 1$, the valid values are:
$$n \in \{2,\; 3,\; 4,\; 6,\; 8,\; 12,\; 24\}$$

That gives **7** values.

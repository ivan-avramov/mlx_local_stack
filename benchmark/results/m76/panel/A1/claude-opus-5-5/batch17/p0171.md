## Prompt
Find $\sin 20^\circ \sin 40^\circ \sin 60^\circ \sin 80^\circ \sin 100^\circ \sin 120^\circ \sin 140^\circ \sin 160^\circ.$

## Response A
# Computing the Product of Sines

## Step 1: Simplify Using Symmetry

Using the identity $\sin(180° - x) = \sin x$:

$$\sin 100° = \sin 80°, \quad \sin 120° = \sin 60°, \quad \sin 140° = \sin 40°, \quad \sin 160° = \sin 20°$$

The product becomes:

$$P = \sin 20° \sin 40° \sin 60° \sin 80° \sin 80° \sin 60° \sin 40° \sin 20° = \bigl(\sin 20° \sin 40° \sin 60° \sin 80°\bigr)^2$$

## Step 2: Evaluate the Inner Product

I use the identity $\sin x \cdot \sin(60°-x) \cdot \sin(60°+x) = \tfrac{1}{4}\sin 3x$.

With $x = 20°$:

$$\sin 20° \cdot \sin 40° \cdot \sin 80° = \frac{1}{4}\sin 60° = \frac{1}{4}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{8}$$

Multiplying by $\sin 60° = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$:

$$\sin 20° \sin 40° \sin 60° \sin 80° = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{8}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = \frac{3}{16}$$

## Step 3: Square the Result

$$P = \left(\frac{3}{16}\right)^2 = \frac{9}{256}$$

$$\boxed{\dfrac{9}{256}}$$

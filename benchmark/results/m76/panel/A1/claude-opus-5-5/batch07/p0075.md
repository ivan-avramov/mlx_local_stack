## Prompt
The lengths of two opposite sides of a square are decreased by $40\%$ while the lengths of the other two sides are increased by $50\%$ to form a rectangle. By what percent does the square's area decrease?

## Response A
# Finding the Percent Decrease in Area

## Setting Up

Let the side length of the square be $s$. The area of the square is:

$$A_{\text{square}} = s^2$$

## Forming the Rectangle

- Two opposite sides are **decreased by 40%**: $s \times (1 - 0.40) = 0.6s$
- The other two sides are **increased by 50%**: $s \times (1 + 0.50) = 1.5s$

The rectangle has dimensions $0.6s \times 1.5s$.

## Computing the Rectangle's Area

$$A_{\text{rectangle}} = 0.6s \times 1.5s = 0.9s^2$$

## Finding the Percent Decrease

$$\text{Decrease} = s^2 - 0.9s^2 = 0.1s^2$$

$$\text{Percent decrease} = \frac{0.1s^2}{s^2} \times 100\% = 10\%$$

$$\boxed{10\%}$$

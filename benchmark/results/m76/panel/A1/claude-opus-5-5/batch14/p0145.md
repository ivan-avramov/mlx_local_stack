## Prompt
Four points, $A$, $B$, $C$, and $D$, are chosen randomly and independently on the circumference of a circle. What is the probability that segments $AB$ and $CD$ intersect?

## Response A
Place the circle’s circumference at length \(1\), and choose points uniformly and independently.

By rotational symmetry, fix \(A\) at position \(0\). Let \(B\) be at position \(x\), where \(x\) is uniformly distributed on \([0,1]\).

The two arcs between \(A\) and \(B\) have lengths

\[
x \quad \text{and} \quad 1-x.
\]

For the chord \(AB\) to intersect the chord \(CD\), the endpoints \(C\) and \(D\) must lie on opposite arcs determined by \(A\) and \(B\). If both lie on the same arc, then the segment \(CD\) does not cross segment \(AB\).

Given \(B\) is at position \(x\), the probability that \(C\) and \(D\) lie on opposite arcs is

\[
2x(1-x).
\]

This is because either:

- \(C\) is on the arc of length \(x\) and \(D\) is on the arc of length \(1-x\), giving probability \(x(1-x)\), or
- \(C\) is on the arc of length \(1-x\) and \(D\) is on the arc of length \(x\), giving another \(x(1-x)\).

Now average over the uniform distribution of \(x\):

\[
\int_0^1 2x(1-x)\,dx
=
2\int_0^1 (x-x^2)\,dx
=
2\left[\frac{x^2}{2}-\frac{x^3}{3}\right]_0^1
=
2\left(\frac12-\frac13\right)
=
\frac13.
\]

Therefore, the probability that segments \(AB\) and \(CD\) intersect is

\[
\boxed{\frac13}
\]

## Response B
By rotational symmetry, fix point \(A\) at angle \(0\). Let \(B\) be chosen randomly. The clockwise arc from \(A\) to \(B\) has fraction

\[
p \in [0,1]
\]

of the full circle, and \(p\) is uniformly distributed on \([0,1]\).

Now consider the other two points \(C\) and \(D\). The chord \(AB\) divides the circle into two arcs of lengths \(p\) and \(1-p\). The chord \(CD\) intersects segment \(AB\) if and only if \(C\) and \(D\) lie on opposite arcs determined by \(A\) and \(B\).

For a fixed \(p\), the probability that \(C\) and \(D\) are on opposite arcs is

\[
2p(1-p).
\]

Therefore the desired probability is

\[
\int_0^1 2p(1-p)\,dp
=2\int_0^1 \left(p-p^2\right)\,dp
=2\left(\frac12-\frac13\right)
=\frac13.
\]

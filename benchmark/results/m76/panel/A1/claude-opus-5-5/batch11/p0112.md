\[
\frac{|x^2-81|}{x^2-36x}<0.
\]

### Step 1: Analyze the numerator

\[
|x^2-81| \ge 0
\]

for every real \(x\). It equals zero when

\[
x^2-81=0
\]

so

\[
x^2=81
\]

\[
x=\pm 9.
\]

Thus,

\[
|x^2-81|>0
\]

for all \(x\neq \pm 9\).

---

### Step 2: Analyze the denominator

Factor the denominator:

\[
x^2-36x=x(x-36).
\]

For the fraction to be negative, since the numerator is nonnegative, the denominator must be negative and the numerator must be positive.

So we need

\[
x(x-36)<0.
\]

This inequality holds when \(x\) is between the roots \(0\) and \(36\):

\[
0<x<36.
\]

---

### Step 3: Exclude points where the numerator is zero

The numerator is zero at

\[
x=-9 \quad \text{and} \quad x=9.
\]

Only \(x=9\) lies in the interval \((0,36)\). At \(x=9\), the fraction equals \(0\), which does not satisfy the strict inequality.

Therefore, we exclude \(x=9\).

---

### Final solution

\[
\boxed{(0,9)\cup(9,36)}
\]
